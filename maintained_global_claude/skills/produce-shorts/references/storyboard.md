# Visual direction (stage 4) and subtitle design (stage 6)

## Stage 4 — Storyboard construction

One **visual director** subagent per approved clip, model = `roles.strong_reasoning`. Directors for different clips run in parallel. Each director receives: the clip's `clip.yaml`, `episode.yaml` (speakers, sync table, probes, platform profiles), the transcript excerpt for the clip, and the storyboard parse contract from `references/schemas.md`.

### A-roll sourcing rules

- Isolated camera files may be used **only** when their `sync` entry has `verified: true`. Unverified sync means unusable footage — the director falls back to source-derived crops, it does not guess offsets.
- Without isolated footage, derive speaker crops from the published split-screen. Respect image quality: a crop must keep the face region at a resolution that upscales to the target frame without visible softness — as a rule, never crop below 55–60% of target width from a 1080p source. When a tight crop would break that, use the source framing or a split-screen instead.
- `preferred_crop` values in `episode.yaml` are the starting point; the director may refine per segment.

### What every output interval must specify

The storyboard covers 100% of output time with no gaps: A-roll or B-roll for every interval; speaker crop / split screen / reaction shot / source framing for A-roll; B-roll **search intent** (what the researcher should find and why it supports the dialogue) and, after synthesis, the selected asset ID; cuts, zooms, reframes, transitions, and motion; on-screen text and graphics; platform-safe zones respected; thumbnail/first-frame text with hierarchy, placement, and fit; aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate (from the platform profile).

Visual pacing guidance: a static talking head longer than ~8–10s usually wants a reframe, zoom nudge, reaction cut, or B-roll overlay — but only where it serves the dialogue. Variation for its own sake is the disease, not the cure; the critic checks for both monotony and distraction.

B-roll `visual.treatment` is a closed set — `cover` (fill frame, crop overflow), `contain` (fit inside, blurred/plain pillars), or `letterbox` (fit on black). The renderer refuses anything else, so directors describe intent in prose but the manifest field must be one of the three.

**Never AI-generated video or imagery.** B-roll comes from configured stock providers or footage the user holds rights to. No exceptions, including "just a background".

### Safe zones (9:16 vertical, 1080x1920 reference)

- Top ~200px: platform UI (captions toggle, sound badge).
- Bottom ~320px: progress bar, description, engagement rail.
- Right ~120px: like/comment/share column.
- Keep subtitles and on-screen text inside the central safe region; faces may extend beyond it, text may not.

### Format

The storyboard is a Markdown doc: title, one-paragraph visual concept, then the **Timeline table** and **Subtitle plan table** in the exact column format defined in `references/schemas.md` (they are machine-parsed), then prose notes (thumbnail spec, graphics, risks). Ranges always, never single ambiguous timestamps.

The director also updates `clip.yaml`: fills `visual` blocks per segment (treatment, motion, transitions), `subtitles.lines`, `thumbnail`, `output`. Run `scripts/validate_clip.py` after every director pass — manifest and storyboard drift is caught here, not at render time.

## Parallel track with stage 5 (assets) + synthesis

The director and the asset researcher work concurrently:

1. Director's first pass marks B-roll segments with `asset_id: null` and a search intent per segment.
2. Researcher (see `references/assets.md`) searches real providers against those intents and returns available, licensable options — or explicit "nothing suitable" per intent.
3. **Synthesis pass** (subagent, model = `roles.strong_reasoning`): reconciles the two. For each B-roll intent, either selects one researched asset (writes `asset_id` into timeline + storyboard, `used_in_segments` onto the asset) or converts the segment to an A-roll treatment when nothing suitable exists. The synthesis never invents assets and never leaves a `broll` segment without a real asset.

## Stage 6 — Subtitle design

Subtitle design happens inside the storyboard (design intent); exact timing is generated **only after the audio edit is locked**, because internal cuts invalidate design-time timestamps. Do not hand-tune subtitle timing in the storyboard — it will be regenerated by forced alignment.

Defaults (overridable in `config/defaults.yaml`):

- **Font:** Inter Semibold (broadcast-safe fallback list in config).
- **Color:** white base; emphasis only from the episode's restrained `emphasis_palette` (1–2 colors max per episode).
- **Emphasis:** weight and color on meaningful words only — the words carrying the idea, not filler. If more than ~15% of words are emphasized, nothing is.
- **Position:** bottom-center default; move to middle-lower only to avoid faces or load-bearing visuals, and record why in the line's `note`.
- **Readability limits (validated by `scripts/validate_subtitles.py`):** ≤2 lines, ≤42 chars/line, ≤20 chars/sec reading speed, ≥0.8s minimum display, phrase boundaries at natural speech breaks (never split a spoken phrase mid-clause to balance line length).
- **Safety:** every rendered line inside platform-safe zones.

### Post-lock subtitle pipeline (stage 8, step 3)

1. `scripts/align_subtitles.py` re-transcribes the rendered audio and force-aligns the manifest's verbatim text to it — word timestamps against the FINAL audio, not the source.
2. Approved phrase breaks and emphasis from `subtitles.lines` are applied to the aligned words.
3. Emits `.ass` with the configured style.
4. `scripts/validate_subtitles.py` checks safe zones, overflow, reading speed, timing gaps/overlaps. A red validation blocks the render composite.
